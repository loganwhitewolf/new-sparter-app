<objective>
Generalize the 1:1 `transaction_pair` model into the locked 1:N reimbursement schema (ADR 0018,
D-01/D-03), migrate existing pairs into it, generalize the shared `effectiveAmount()` /
`isNotSecondary()` netting fragment to read the new tables (D-05/D-06), and prove — with a real,
runnable regression test against a real Postgres — that the narrowest possible case (one migrated
1:1 pair, N=1) produces byte-identical results before and after across all 10 verified aggregation
call sites that consume the shared fragment (not just top-line dashboard totals — see Task 3 for
the full, source-verified list, which corrects and expands 73-RESEARCH.md's original 8-function
inventory). This is this phase's tracer: every layer the phase touches (schema, migration, shared
netting SQL, aggregation call sites) gets wired end-to-end for the thinnest slice before Plan 73-02
expands the proof matrix.

Purpose: prove the architecture holds before investing in the full N-refund matrix, the downstream
consumer repoints, and the live write-path repoint — exactly the risk ADR 0018 and 73-RESEARCH.md
flag as "the sharp edge" of this phase.
Output: `reimbursement` + `reimbursement_refund` tables, a migration that backfills every existing
`transaction_pair` row, a generalized `effectiveAmount()`/`isNotSecondary()`, and a regression
harness proving N=1 correctness.

**Flagged assumptions carried from the spec-less probe fallback** (RMB-01, RMB-03, RMB-05 came back
`unclassified` from the deterministic probe — per protocol they are surfaced here explicitly, not
silently resolved): this plan's `must_haves.truths` and Plan 73-02's invariant tests are this
project's concrete answer to those three, sourced from ADR 0018 D-01/D-02/D-06 directly rather than
from the generic probe. RMB-04's three classified probes (empty/adjacency/ordering) are split
across this plan (empty/N=1) and Plan 73-02 (adjacency, ordering, dinner N=3, Q3 multi-transaction
Expense).
</objective>
